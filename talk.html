Talk to someone.

You don’t need to be in a crisis to call. If you’ve felt off for a while and can’t shake it, that’s reason enough. Below are free helplines, and some ways to bring it up with people you already know.

If you or someone else is in danger right now, call 112.

Free helplines in India

All of these are free to use. You can share as little or as much as you like, and if one line is busy, it’s okay to try another.

  • Tele-MANAS

    The Government of India’s mental health helpline.

    not sure which one to call? start here.

    14416 or 1-800-891-4416

    Toll-free · 24×7 · choose your language

    Sources: Tele-MANAS website (opens in a new tab), launch, PIB 2022 (opens in a new tab), PIB 2024 (opens in a new tab)

  • Vandrevala Foundation

    Trained counsellors. Call or WhatsApp, from anywhere in India.

    +91 9999 666 555

    Free · 24×7 · private and confidential · call or WhatsApp

    Source: vandrevalafoundation.com (opens in a new tab)

  • iCALL

    Professional counsellors from TISS, Mumbai. You can stay anonymous.

    9152987821

    Free · Monday to Saturday, 10 am to 8 pm · also by email: icall@tiss.edu

    Source: TISS iCALL page (opens in a new tab)

  • AASRA

    For anyone in emotional crisis. Calls aren’t recorded and your identity is never shared.

    022 2754 6669

    Free · 24×7 · English and Hindi

    Source: aasra.info (opens in a new tab)

  • Child Helpline

    If you’re under 18 and unsafe, at home or anywhere else.

    1098

    Toll-free · 24×7 · connected to 112

    Source: Ministry of Women and Child Development, PIB 2025 (opens in a new tab)

Toll-free numbers cost nothing to call. The other services are free, but your phone plan’s usual call or data charges may apply.

Not in India?

Find A Helpline (opens in a new tab) lists free, confidential helplines in more than 175 countries. You can also search for “mental health helpline” with the name of your country or city, or ask a school counsellor or doctor what’s available near you.

Numbers checked on 16 October 2026. Services sometimes change; if a number doesn’t connect, try another one or call 112.

Telling someone you know

You don’t need a speech. A text, a note left on their desk, or bringing it up in the car, where nobody has to make eye contact: all of these count.

A parent

Pick a calm moment, not right before an exam or in the middle of an argument. They might not have perfect words, and that’s okay. Most parents would much rather know.

“Can I talk to you about something? I’ve been finding things really hard lately, and it’s not just about marks.”

A friend

You don’t need a big reason. Friends often feel the same way and are relieved someone said it first.

“Honestly, I haven’t been okay. Can we talk later?”

A teacher or counsellor

Choose someone you feel safe with. A school counsellor is there exactly for this, and it isn’t only for “serious” problems.

“Could I talk to you after class? Something’s been bothering me and I don’t know who else to ask.”

If the first person brushes it off, try someone else, or one of the numbers above. A bad first reaction doesn’t mean you were wrong to say it.

If a friend is struggling

You don’t have to fix anything. Taking them seriously is most of it.

  1. Reach out

    If they seem quiet or not like themselves, say something simple. “You seemed off today. You okay?” And if they say “fine”, ask twice.

  2. Listen more than you talk

    Let them finish. You don’t need advice or a story of your own. “That sounds really hard. I’m glad you told me” goes a long way.

  3. Help them find support

    Gently suggest talking to a trusted adult, a counsellor or a helpline. Offer to sit with them while they call, or go with them to talk to someone.

  4. Don’t keep it a secret if they’re unsafe

    If they talk about not wanting to be alive or about hurting themselves, take it seriously. Stay with them if you can, and tell a trusted adult or call one of the numbers above. You can be a good friend and still tell an adult.

  5. Look after yourself too

    Supporting someone can feel heavy. It’s okay to talk to someone about how you’re feeling as well.

Whenever you’re ready.
