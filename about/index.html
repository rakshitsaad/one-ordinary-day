About this site

One Ordinary Day is a short, interactive story about what teenagers often carry quietly, and what can help. It follows Aarav, 17, through one school day, from his third snooze to lights out, with small exercises and free help along the way.

Who it’s for

Students in India having a day like Aarav’s: boards, coaching, comparison, a family that cares a lot. And the friends, parents and teachers around them, who might want to understand what it’s like from the inside.

What’s real and what isn’t

  • Aarav, Kabir and his family are made up. Their day is put together from things many students describe.
  • The helpline numbers are real. They were checked against each service’s official website or the Government of India in October 2026, and each one is linked to its source on the Talk to someone page.
  • Every number in the story links to where it comes from, such as WHO, UNICEF or a government survey.
  • Nothing here is a test or a diagnosis. The exercises are small, everyday things to try, and they’re no replacement for talking to someone.

Your privacy

This site doesn’t ask who you are. It has no accounts, no ads, and no analytics or tracking code. Nothing you click, pick or type is sent anywhere.

  • Anything you write here, in Untangle a thought, the 5-4-3-2-1 notebook or A note for tonight, stays on your device. It’s never sent to us or anyone else.
  • Nothing you write is saved unless you choose “Keep my notes on this device” in Untangle a thought. Then your notes are kept in this browser, on this device only, and “Delete my notes” removes them straight away. “Start over” clears the screen but keeps notes you chose to keep, until you delete them.
  • Which thought you pick, and your answer to “Does this feel more manageable?”, aren’t kept anywhere.
  • The 5-4-3-2-1 notebook and A note for tonight never save what you type. They’re cleared when you leave the page.
  • The library’s “I feel…” buttons only change what’s highlighted on the page. Your choice isn’t kept.
  • The small garden in the corner only remembers that you finished an exercise, and when, in this browser. You can clear it from the garden.
  • If you’re on a shared or school computer, it’s best not to save your notes.

A gentle note

This site talks about stress, anxiety and feeling low. Go at your own pace, and stop whenever you like. If you’re in danger right now, call 112. If you’d like to talk to someone, the free helplines are a good place to start.

Whenever you’re ready.
