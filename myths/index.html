Myths we grew up with.

Usually from people who loved us and meant well. Tap a note to see what’s actually true.

  • myth

    “It’s just a phase.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    Some moods do pass. But if sadness or worry hangs around for weeks and gets in the way of sleep, school or friends, it deserves real attention, not a wait-and-see. Asking early is never an overreaction.

  • myth

    “Therapy is for crazy people.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    Therapy is for anyone carrying something heavy: exam stress, a breakup, a fight at home, a mood you can’t explain. It’s like seeing a doctor for a sprain. You don’t wait until you can’t walk.

  • myth

    “Boys don’t cry.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    Everyone cries. Boys are usually taught to swallow it, so they carry the same feelings with nowhere to put them. Most of the men you know have cried too. They just didn’t let you see.

  • myth

    “Just think positive and it’ll go away.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    If it worked like that, nobody would struggle. Feelings aren’t a switch you flip by trying harder. What helps is real support: someone who listens, rest, and sometimes a professional. “Just be happy” usually makes people feel more alone.

  • myth

    “What stress? You’re just a kid.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    Ranks, comparison, friendships, expectations at home: teenage stress is real stress. Around 1 in 7 people aged 10 to 19 lives with a mental health condition (WHO, opens in a new tab). Being young doesn’t make it small.

  • myth

    “Talking about it will only make it worse.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    Saying it out loud usually makes it lighter, not heavier. Being listened to, even once, can turn a spiral into something you can actually look at. You don’t need the perfect words. “Not really, honestly” is enough.

  • myth

    “Strong people handle it alone.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    The people who seem strongest usually have someone they call when it gets bad. Asking for help is a skill, and like any skill, the second time is easier than the first.

  • myth

    “Sleep is for after the exams.”

    tap to flip
    actually…

    Teenagers need 8 to 10 hours of sleep a night (CDC, opens in a new tab). A tired brain makes everything harder: focus, mood, memory. Revising at 2 am feels productive; the trouble is remembering any of it the next day.
